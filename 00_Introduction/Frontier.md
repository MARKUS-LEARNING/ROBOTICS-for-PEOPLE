## Subfields with fewer than three notes

Robotics is wider than any table of contents. These parts of it are barely covered yet:

- **Systems Engineering, Safety and Testing** (2 notes): Integrating everything into a robot that works, is safe, and can be tested, verified and maintained.
- **Aerial Robotics** (1 note): Drones, multirotors and fixed-wing UAVs, aerial manipulation.
- **Soft and Continuum Robotics** (2 notes): Robots made of compliant materials, continuum and cable-driven bodies.
- **Bio-inspired Robotics** (2 notes): Borrowing from animals and plants: biomimetic design, morphology and locomotion.
- **Field Robotics** (1 note): Robots outdoors and in extreme environments: mining, construction, disaster response, inspection.
- **Agricultural Robotics** (1 note): Planting, weeding, harvesting and monitoring crops and livestock.
- **Marine and Underwater Robotics** (1 note): AUVs, ROVs and surface vessels.
- **Space Robotics** (1 note): Planetary rovers, orbital manipulators and in-space assembly.
- **Assistive Robotics, Prosthetics and Exoskeletons** (1 note): Robots that restore or extend human ability: exoskeletons, prostheses, rehabilitation and care robots.
- **Construction and Architecture Robotics** (0 notes): Robots that build: additive construction, bricklaying, site layout, large-scale fabrication.
- **Robots in Art, Entertainment and Education** (2 notes): Animatronics, robot art and performance, toys, and robots as teaching tools.
- **Multi-Robot Systems and Swarms** (2 notes): Many robots at once: coordination, distributed control, swarm intelligence.
