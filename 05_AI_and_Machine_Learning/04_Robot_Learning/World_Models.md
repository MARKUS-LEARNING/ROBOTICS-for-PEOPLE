# World Models

A **world model** is a learned model of how the world changes. Given what the robot sees now and an action it might take, it predicts what will happen next: the next state, the next camera frame, or the next few seconds of video. With such a model, a robot can *imagine* the consequences of its actions before taking them, much as a person pictures a glass tipping before reaching past it.

In the language of [[Markov_Decision_Process|MDPs]], a world model learns the transition function $p(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t)$ (and often the reward) from data, instead of having it written by hand as in a physics simulator.

> **Etymology.** The idea is older than the term. Psychologist Kenneth Craik argued in *The Nature of Explanation* (1943) that the mind carries "a small-scale model of external reality" to try out alternatives before acting. "World model" was common in 1970s–80s AI planning; David Ha and Jürgen Schmidhuber's 2018 paper *World Models* made it the standard name for learned, neural dynamics models.

## What a world model is used for

| Use | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning** | Search over action sequences inside the model, pick the best, execute the first step, repeat | Latent-space model-predictive control; V-JEPA 2 planning to reach image goals |
| **Training in imagination** | Train a policy on rollouts generated by the model instead of the real world | Dreamer family; DayDreamer learned a quadruped gait on hardware in about an hour |
| **Synthetic data** | Generate varied, realistic video for training perception or policies | Video world-model platforms such as Cosmos |
| **Policy evaluation** | Run a policy inside the model to estimate success before real tests | An active 2025–26 research use |
| **Subgoal generation** | Imagine an image of the goal state and give it to the policy | π0.7 can be prompted with generated subgoal images |
| **Acting directly** | Generate future video and actions together | World action models (below) |

## Three generations

**1. Latent dynamics models (2018–).** Encode observations into a compact latent state $z_t$, learn $p(z_{t+1} \mid z_t, a_t)$ and a reward predictor, and train a policy on imagined latent trajectories. Ha and Schmidhuber (2018) learned to play a car-racing game this way; **Dreamer** (Hafner et al. 2019, 2023) made it a general reinforcement learning method, and DayDreamer (Wu et al. 2022) applied it to physical robots.

**2. Video world models (2024–).** Large video generators trained on enormous amounts of footage of the physical world, conditioned on text or actions. Examples include NVIDIA's Cosmos platform (2025), Google DeepMind's interactive Genie models and Meta's V-JEPA 2 (2025), which predicts in an abstract representation space rather than pixels and was used to plan robot actions toward goal images.

**3. World action models (2026–).** Generate future video *and* robot actions together, using a pretrained video model as the backbone. NVIDIA's **DreamZero** (Ye et al., February 2026) is a 14-billion-parameter model built on an image-to-video diffusion model; the authors report over twice the generalisation of leading VLAs to new tasks and environments on real robots. NVIDIA's **GR00T 2** (research release, November 2026) is a 2-billion-parameter world action model built on Cosmos-Predict2.5; NVIDIA reports 40% zero-shot success on 50 tasks unseen on the target robot, rising to 95% with five demonstrations and a 30-minute fine-tune. These are vendor-reported numbers.

## Model-based planning in one equation

Given a learned model $\hat f$ and reward $\hat r$, plan over a horizon $H$:

$$a^*_{t:t+H} = \arg\max_{a_{t:t+H}} \sum_{k=0}^{H} \hat r(\hat s_{t+k}, a_{t+k}), \qquad \hat s_{t+k+1} = \hat f(\hat s_{t+k}, a_{t+k})$$

Execute $a^*_t$, observe the real next state, and plan again. This is [[Model_Predictive_Control_(MPC)|model-predictive control]] with a learned model in place of hand-derived equations.

## Worked example: why horizons stay short

Suppose a model's prediction is 1% off at each step, and errors compound multiplicatively. After $k$ steps the accumulated error is about $1.01^k - 1$:

| Steps imagined | Accumulated error |
|---|---|
| 10 | ≈ 10% |
| 50 | ≈ 64% |
| 100 | ≈ 170% |

So imagined rollouts are kept short (tens of steps), plans are re-made every step from fresh observations, and policies trained in imagination must eventually be checked against reality. A policy may also learn to **exploit** model errors, finding "solutions" that only work inside the flawed model; this is the learned-model version of the sim-to-real gap.

## World models versus physics simulators

| | Physics simulator | Learned world model |
|---|---|---|
| Built from | Equations of motion, contact models | Data (video, robot logs) |
| Strength | Exact, controllable, cheap per step | Captures appearance and messy real effects (cloth, liquids, people) |
| Weakness | Hard to model soft, deformable, visual complexity | Can hallucinate; hard to guarantee physical consistency |
| Typical use | RL for locomotion, contact-rich tasks | Data generation, evaluation, generalist policies |

The two are converging: physics simulators borrow learned components, and learned models are increasingly trained or checked against simulators.

## State of the art (as of November 2026)

- World action models (DreamZero, GR00T 2) are the newest family of generalist robot policies, competing with [[Vision-Language-Action_Models]] that predict actions alone.
- Video world models are used to generate training data, to evaluate policies, and to produce subgoal images that steer policies (as in π0.7).
- Training reinforcement learning policies inside learned world models is an active research direction, not yet standard practice.
